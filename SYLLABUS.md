## Description

Understanding the world and making sound policy decisions requires engaging critically with data. Yet real-world data is often messy, incomplete, and difficult to interpret. Learning to navigate these challenges enables students to cut through rhetoric, test assumptions, assess competing claims, and ground policy decisions in evidence.

This intensive seminar is designed to build students’ confidence working with data and develop two core skills:

* **Critically evaluating quantitative evidence.** Students will learn how to interrogate the reports, statistics, indicators, and datasets that shape public debate: where the data comes from, how it was produced, what its limitations are, and whether the conclusions drawn from it are justified. They will develop the judgment needed to spot misleading claims, question assumptions, and assess the strength of quantitative evidence.
* **Using data to investigate policy questions.** Students will gain hands-on experience using real-world data and modern analytical tools to explore substantive policy questions. They will learn how to formulate questions that can be investigated with data, work through the limitations of imperfect datasets, interpret results responsibly, and translate complex evidence into clear and actionable insights.

The seminar will be offered at two levels (Intermediate and Advanced) to accommodate students with different levels of quantitative experience. Both versions will follow a similar structure and develop the same core skills, but will differ in the depth and sophistication of the analytical methods introduced and applied. The Intermediate seminar will emphasize accessible methods and build a strong foundation for working confidently with data, while the Advanced seminar will engage with more complex analytical techniques and their application. Students will self-select into their preferred stream on the first day of the Complexity Seminar, with guidance from course instructors as needed to ensure that their choice aligns with their prior experience, learning goals, and level of quantitative proficiency.

## Student assessment & expectations

Students will be evaluated based on their participation, completion of daily assignments, and final research project.

* **Daily Participation (15%; Level 1: AI-free)** – Students are expected to actively engage in class discussions, practical exercises, and peer feedback throughout the seminar.
* **Daily Project Check-Ins (15%; Level 1: AI-free)** – A list of project milestones will be posted on myCourses each day, outlining the expected progress toward completion of the final project. Students will be required to complete a daily checklist indicating which milestones they have and have not yet achieved, respond to a series of short reflection prompts about their progress, challenges, and next steps, and post their responses to the designated myCourses discussion board. These check-ins are intended to encourage consistent progress, promote self-reflection, and help identify areas where additional guidance or support may be needed. **All submissions must be completed individually and posted before the beginning of the next class day.**
* **Final Research Project (70%)** – The final project consists of two components:
  * **Mini-Conference Presentation (40%; Level 1: AI-free):** A 3–5 minute poster-style presentation or panel presentation, supported by one or two figures, communicating the research question, methodology, key findings, and policy implications.
  * **Final Write-Up (30%; Level 3: AI-expected):** A 1,500-word written report presenting the research question, data sources, analytical approach, principal findings, limitations, and policy implications. The report should demonstrate clear reasoning, appropriate methodological choices, and effective communication of evidence. AI is allowed.
    * As an appendix to the report, students must also submit a Reproducible Project Workspace that allows their analytical work to be reviewed and reproduced. Submission requirements differ by stream:
      * **Intermediate Stream:** A clean, organized Excel workbook containing `raw`, `working`, and `notes` tabs, documenting the original data, analytical steps, and relevant methodological decisions.
      * **Advanced Stream:** A reproducible project folder or Git repository containing a `README.md` file, data dictionary, and analytical scripts, with sufficient documentation to allow others to understand and reproduce the analysis.

## Day 1 – From policy questions to research-ready projects

### Learning Objectives
By the end of Day 1, students will be able to:

* Formulate a clear, empirically answerable policy research question
* Identify appropriate analytical methods and understand the types of claims each can support
* Evaluate data provenance, quality, and suitability for policy research
* Navigate curated datasets and publicly available Canadian data sources
* Establish a reproducible research workflow using Claude Code
* Assess the feasibility of a proposed research project, including data acquisition and linkage considerations where applicable

### 1. Foundations of Policy Research (Intermediate & Advanced Streams)

#### *From Policy Problems to Answerable Research Questions*
* What makes a policy question empirically answerable?
* Defining populations, outcomes, exposures, geographic scope, and time horizons
* Distinguishing policy relevance from analytical feasibility
* Refining broad policy interests into specific research questions

#### *Overview of Analytical Methods*
* Introduction to common quantitative approaches used in policy research
* Matching research questions to appropriate methodologies
* Understanding the types of claims supported by descriptive, comparative, correlational, predictive, and causal approaches
* Recognizing the assumptions and limitations of different research designs

#### *Data Provenance and Research Feasibility*
* Understanding who collected the data, how it was generated, and for what purpose
* Evaluating data coverage, representativeness, variable definitions, completeness, and limitations
* Assessing whether available data can credibly answer a proposed research question
* Identifying when research questions must be refined based on data constraints

### 2. Research Workflow & Tools (Parallel Intermediate & Advanced Streams)

#### *Research Documentation and Reproducibility*
* The research log: why documenting the analytical process is as important as the final output
* Recording research decisions, assumptions, data transformations, and analytical outputs
* Tracking, reviewing, and validating AI-generated code, particularly when working with Claude Code
* Establishing transparent and reproducible research practices

#### *Navigating Research Data*
* Tour of the seminar's curated datasets
* Introduction to key Canadian public data repositories:
  * Statistics Canada tables
  * Open Government Portal ([open.canada.ca](http://open.canada.ca))
  * Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC) data portal
* Locating datasets, reviewing metadata, and identifying relevant variables
* Assessing dataset suitability for specific research questions

#### *Project Setup and Introduction to Claude Code*
* Establishing a consistent project folder structure
* Organizing raw data, processed data, code, documentation, and outputs
* Initializing a project and completing a first Claude Code session
* Using Claude Code for preliminary data inspection and exploration
* Reviewing generated code and documenting the analytical process

#### ***Intermediate Stream** – Data Management with Excel*
* Importing and organizing datasets in Excel
* Understanding data structures, variable types, and consistent formatting
* Cleaning and preparing data, including handling missing values, duplicates, and inconsistent entries
* Using sorting, filtering, formulas, and lookup functions to manage and combine data
* Conducting preliminary data validation and quality checks
* Documenting data cleaning decisions and maintaining reproducible workflows

#### ***Advanced Stream** – Data Acquisition, Integration & Version Control*
* Identifying and accessing data beyond curated sources, including APIs, bulk downloads, and web scraping
* Understanding responsible data acquisition practices and evaluating source reliability
* Assessing the feasibility of combining multiple datasets, including compatibility of geographic, temporal, and variable definitions
* Identifying potential linkage variables and developing preliminary data integration strategies
* Setting up and managing a Git repository for version control
* Tracking changes, creating commits, and maintaining a reproducible project history
* Integrating Git and Claude Code into a transparent, reproducible research workflow

## Day 2 – Explore, clean & align

### Learning Objectives
By the end of Day 2, students will be able to:

* Conduct preliminary exploratory data analysis to assess data quality and identify patterns
* Identify and address missing values, outliers, inconsistencies, and other data quality issues
* Understand how data cleaning decisions influence analytical results and document these decisions transparently
* Evaluate the comparability of measures across populations, geographies, and time periods
* Use Claude Code to explore, clean, and structure datasets for analysis
* Identify and resolve data alignment challenges, including cross-source integration and geographic inconsistencies where applicable

### 1. Foundations of Data Exploration and Preparation (Intermediate & Advanced Streams)

#### *Exploratory Data Analysis: Understanding the Data*
* Conducting an initial assessment of dataset structure, dimensions, and variable types
* Examining distributions, summary statistics, missing values, and potential outliers
* Identifying unexpected patterns, inconsistencies, and potential data quality concerns
* Distinguishing between genuine variation and potential data errors

#### *Data Cleaning as an Analytical Decision*
* Understanding how data cleaning choices influence research findings
* Identifying and addressing missing values, duplicate observations, inconsistent formats, and outliers
* Evaluating the implications of excluding observations, recoding variables, or modifying datasets
* Documenting cleaning decisions, assumptions, and their rationale in the research log

#### *Aligning Measures Across Datasets*
* Understanding denominators, baselines, and units of measurement
* Evaluating consistency across geographic units, populations, and time periods
* Identifying changes in variable definitions, data collection practices, and reporting standards
* Making meaningful cross-group comparisons

### 2. Data Exploration & Preparation Tools (Parallel Intermediate & Advanced Streams)

#### *Understanding Dataset Documentation*
* Reading and interpreting codebooks, data dictionaries, and metadata
* Identifying variable definitions, coding schemes, units of measurement, and missing value conventions
* Recognizing potential limitations and inconsistencies in dataset documentation

#### *Structuring Research Data*
* Introduction to tidy data principles: observations, variables, and values
* Distinguishing between wide and long data formats
* Understanding how data structure affects cleaning, visualization, and analysis
* Preparing datasets for consistent and reproducible analytical workflows

#### *Exploratory Data Analysis with Claude Code*
* Using Claude Code to generate descriptive statistics and assess data quality
* Examining variable distributions through histograms and other basic visualizations
* Exploring trends over time using time-series plots
* Identifying missingness, outliers, and unexpected patterns
* Reviewing AI-generated outputs for accuracy and documenting analytical decisions

#### ***Intermediate Stream** – Data Cleaning & Exploration with Excel and Claude Code*
* Importing and organizing datasets for cleaning and exploratory analysis
* Using Excel to identify missing values, duplicates, inconsistent formats, and potential outliers
* Applying formulas, filters, and basic data validation techniques to clean variables
* Standardizing dates, categories, geographic identifiers, and units of measurement
* Using Claude Code to support exploratory analysis and generate initial visualizations
* Identifying and resolving measurement alignment issues within individual project datasets
* Documenting cleaning and transformation decisions in the research log
* **Individual project clinic:** Reviewing each student's dataset, addressing data cleaning and alignment challenges, and refining the proposed analytical approach

#### ***Advanced Stream** – Data Integration, Harmonization & Code Auditing*
* Joining multiple datasets using common identifiers and geographic concordance tables
* Addressing geographic boundary changes, inconsistent reporting units, and differences in observation levels
* Harmonizing variable definitions, time periods, and measurement units across sources
* Evaluating the implications of unmatched records, duplicate keys, and many-to-many joins
* Auditing AI-generated cleaning and transformation code for accuracy, completeness, and unintended data loss
* Implementing validation checks to verify data transformations and integration results
* Documenting integration decisions, assumptions, and limitations to ensure reproducibility
* **Individual project clinic:** Reviewing each student's data integration strategy, troubleshooting linkage and harmonization challenges, and refining the proposed analytical approach

## Day 3 – Analytical Methods & Applications

### Learning Objectives
By the end of Day 3, students will be able to:

* Apply a range of quantitative methods to describe patterns, compare populations, and evaluate policy-relevant outcomes
* Understand the strengths, assumptions, and limitations of different analytical approaches
* Distinguish between descriptive findings, associations, and causal claims
* Use Claude Code to implement and interpret analytical methods
* Select an appropriate methodology for their individual research project based on the research question, available data, and intended claims
* Explore more advanced modelling and AI-assisted analytical techniques where applicable

### 1. Foundations of Quantitative Policy Analysis (Intermediate & Advanced Streams)

#### *The Regression-Free Analytical Toolkit*
* Comparing means, proportions, and distributions across populations or time periods
* Calculating rates, ratios, and standardized measures to support meaningful comparisons
* Measuring concentrations and relative intensities using location quotients and related indicators
* Constructing indices and evaluating changes relative to a base year
* Selecting appropriate measures based on the research question, data structure, and policy context

#### *Interpreting Evidence and Understanding Causality*
* Distinguishing descriptive patterns, statistical associations, and causal relationships
* Understanding what different analytical methods can and cannot establish
* Recognizing the role of assumptions, confounding factors, and alternative explanations
* Identifying when causal inference methods are required
* Communicating findings accurately without overstating their implications

### 2. Applied Analytical Methods & Tools (Parallel Intermediate & Advanced Streams)

#### *Descriptive & Relational Analysis*
* Comparing groups, geographic areas, and time periods using summary statistics, rates, and indices
* Exploring relationships between variables through cross-tabulations, correlations, and visualizations
* Interpreting patterns and identifying limitations in descriptive and relational evidence

#### *Scoring & Synthesis*
* Combining multiple indicators into composite scores or indices
* Understanding normalization, weighting, and aggregation methods
* Evaluating how methodological choices influence rankings and composite measures

#### *Accounting Frameworks*
* Using accounting identities and decomposition approaches to understand changes in policy-relevant outcomes
* Breaking aggregate measures into their underlying components
* Applying structured accounting frameworks to questions involving populations, resources, or economic activity

#### *Simulation*
* Developing simple scenario-based models to explore potential policy outcomes
* Defining model inputs, assumptions, and relationships
* Comparing alternative scenarios and interpreting model outputs

#### *Probabilistic Analysis*
* Introducing uncertainty through probability distributions and stochastic assumptions
* Using simple probabilistic models and Monte Carlo simulation to explore possible outcomes
* Interpreting ranges, probabilities, and sensitivity to model assumptions

#### *Implementing Methods with Claude Code*
* Guided demonstration of one practical application from each methodological category
* Using Claude Code to generate calculations, analytical outputs, and visualizations
* Validating AI-generated analyses and checking assumptions
* Documenting methodological choices and interpretations in the research log

#### ***Intermediate Stream** – Method Selection & Applied Analysis*
* Applying descriptive and comparative methods to policy-relevant datasets
* Calculating and interpreting rates, standardized measures, indices, and summary statistics
* Using Claude Code to implement selected analytical methods and generate interpretable outputs
* Assessing whether methodological assumptions are appropriate for the available data
* Evaluating the strengths and limitations of different approaches for answering specific policy questions
* Documenting analytical decisions and avoiding unsupported causal interpretations
* **Individual project clinic:** Selecting an appropriate method for each student's research question, assessing its feasibility with the available data, and developing an initial analytical plan

#### ***Advanced Stream** – Analytical Frameworks, Modelling & AI-Assisted Methods*
* Selecting and adapting analytical methods to more complex research questions and data structures
* Developing informal models and analytical frameworks based on explicit assumptions and hypothesized relationships
* Translating conceptual frameworks into quantitative calculations and scenario-based analyses
* Assessing model sensitivity to assumptions, parameter choices, and alternative scenarios
* Demonstrating LLM-assisted classification and text analysis, including categorizing unstructured information and extracting policy-relevant themes
* Evaluating the reliability, reproducibility, and limitations of LLM-generated classifications
* **Individual project clinic:** Providing targeted methodological support, refining analytical frameworks, and evaluating the feasibility and limitations of proposed approaches

#### *Developing Policy Briefs and Presentations*

* Adapting the level of technical detail to the intended audience

#### ***Intermediate Stream** – Data Visualization & Policy Brief Development*

* Selecting appropriate chart types and refining figures generated with Claude Code
* Improving figure titles, labels, annotations, and source documentation
* Identifying the key messages that should be emphasized in a policy brief

## Day 5 – Student mini-conference

The final presentation day will be structured as a conference, consisting of three 2-hour blocks. Each student will be assigned to one block, during which their attendance and participation are mandatory. Students are welcome and encouraged to attend the other blocks to learn about their peers' work and engage in discussion. Attendance at the final block of the day is mandatory for all students, regardless of their assigned presentation block.

The conference will be organized as follows:

* **Blocks 1 and 2 – Poster Presentations (Intermediate Students):** Intermediate students will present their projects in a poster-style format. Each student will deliver a brief 3–5 minute presentation, supported by one or two figures illustrating their principal findings. Presentations will be followed by opportunities for questions, discussion, and constructive peer feedback.
* **Block 3 – Panel Discussions (Advanced Students):** Advanced students will present their projects through moderated panel discussions. This format will encourage deeper engagement with the research, including discussion of methodological choices, findings, policy implications, and connections across projects. **All students are required to attend this block.**

### Presentation Guidelines
* Presentations should introduce the policy question, data sources, analytical approach, key findings, and policy implications.
* Students should prioritize clear communication of their central message rather than comprehensive technical detail.
* Students should be prepared to answer questions, engage in discussion, and provide constructive feedback on their peers' work.
